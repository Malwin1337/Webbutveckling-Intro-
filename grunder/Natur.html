The Baltic Coast

  • The Baltic Coast is a unique brackish inland sea region in Northern Europe, bordered by nine countries, featuring rocky archipelagos in the north and flat, sandy shores in the south. You can navigate thousands of pine-topped granite islands by ferry or kayak, or hike segments of the 279 kilometer Stockholm Archipelago trail.
  • Visit the UNESCO World Heritage medieval town of Visby on Gotland, featuring 13th-century stone walls, cobblestone lanes, and rose-covered ruins.
  • Discover dramatic geological wonders: experience the High Coast (Höga kusten) in the north, a UNESCO site where post-glacial land uplift created the highest islands and sheerest coastal cliffs in the Baltic region.
  • Enjoy unspoiled solitude: escape the crowds in regions like Hälsingland, where deep pine forest meets a rocky eastern coast dotted with over 500 quiet islands and traditional fishing hamlets.

Dense Forest and Peaceful Lakes

Around 70% of Sweden is covered by forest, but most of it are mainly consits of pine and spruce. The country is also home to almost 100 000 lakes, including lake Vänern and lake vättern, which are among the largest in Europe.

Mountains and the Frozen North

In the north and along the Norwegian border, you will find impressive mountain ranges(Sken). Lapland is famous for its wild tundra, snowy winters, and unique natural phenomena. A lesser know fact is that Lapland covers the countries of Norway, Sweden, Finland and Russia.
