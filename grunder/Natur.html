The Baltic Coast

  • The Baltic Coast is a unique brakish inland sea region in Northern Europe boarded by nine countries , featuring rocky Archipelagoes in the north and flat, sandy shores in the south

    You can navigate thousends of pine-topped granite islands by ferry or kayak, or hike segments of the 279 kilometers Stockholm Archipelagoe trail.
  • Visit the UNESCO World Heritage medieval town of Visby on Gotland, featuring 13th-century stone walls, cobbelstone lanes, and rose covered ruins
  • Discover Dramatic Geological Wonders: Experience the High Coast (Höga kusten) in the north, a UNESCO site where post-glacial land uplift created the highest islands and sheerest coastal cliffs in the Baltic region
  • Enjoy unspoled solitude: Escape the crowds in regions like Hälsingland, where deep pine forest meet a rocky eastern coast dotted with over 500 quiet islands and traditional fishing hamlets.

Dense Forest and Peaceful Lakes

Around 70% of Sweden is covered by forest, but most of it are mainly consits of pine and spruce. The country is also home to almost 100 000 lakes, including lake Vänern and lake vättern, which are among the largest in Europe.

Mountains and the Frozen North

In the north and along the Norwegian border, you will find impressive mountain ranges(Sken). Lapland is famous for its wild tundra, snowy winters, and unique natural phenomena. A lesser know fact is that Lapland covers the countries of Norway, Sweden, Finland and Russia.

The coral cave in Jämtland

The coral cave got discoverd by humans in the 1985, and it has taken around thousends of years of water constant hit the rock to form limestone-rich rock. Is has an impressive lenght of six kilometers, which makes it one of the largets caves in Sweden. The only way to explore the cave is a guidet tour.
